Supplier changes surface while the plan can still absorb them

Fortune 500 manufacturer · Material planning · JD Edwards stays the system of record
The decision

The re-plan call
Before

At-risk orders get worked before the customer notices

European home furnishings retailer · Supply planning · 75,000 supplier orders a year
The decision
Which supplier orders need intervention before the date promised to the customer
Still human
Escalations and the customer call
Before
  • Supplier delays and missing confirmations found too late to act
  • Late fixes became store escalations, commercial gestures and cancellations
  • Planners’ time went to firefighting
After
Seven alert types, each with a rule for when it opens and when it closes:
  1. No confirmation 48 hours after the order
  2. Delivery visibility for the next 15 days
  3. Supplier late against the confirmed date
  4. No delivery appointment 72 hours out
  5. Partial backorder: needs a date for the rest, or a cancellation
  6. Escalation to a planner after three unanswered follow-ups
  7. Customer promise at risk: the supplier’s new date lands after the customer’s delivery date
Follow-up emails drafted automatically; the planner approves.

Buyers start the day with the SKUs that need a decision

Dorothy Gaynor · Footwear and accessories retail, Mexico · 2,000+ SKUs across 143 stores
The decision
Which SKUs to reorder (and in which sizes and quantities), and which slow movers to act on, every morning
Still human
The buy
Before
  • Buyers reviewed the first 5 to 6 pages of top sellers each week; the rest went unwatched
  • A new shoe sold 45% of its initial inventory in month one, and the reorder came too late
  • $40K to $50K a month spent moving unsold stock between stores and warehouses
After
  • Read-only connection to sales, inventory and open POs, analyzed daily
  • A prioritized morning brief: reorders, rising sellers, slow movers
  • Reorder recommendations with quantities and size breakdowns, based on sales velocity and lead times
  • Phantom stock detection; questions about any SKU or store answered in Spanish
  • Buyers set their own lead times, thresholds and critical products
